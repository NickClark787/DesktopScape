# Inferno arena renderer (`renderer/inferno/render`)

The visual layer for the TzKal-Zuk (wave 69) fight. **Rendering-only**: it
reads engine state and draws it. No tick timing, glyph geometry, attack
pattern or RNG lives here — all of that is in
[`src/sim/tzkalZuk`](../../../sim/tzkalZuk/README.md).

The loop, camera, interpolation, effect pools, quality tiers and perf
budget come from the shared
[arena render core](../../arena/README.md); this package supplies only
what is specific to the Inferno.

| File | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `palette.ts` | volcanic colours + the Inferno's gradients |
| `arenaLayer.ts` | the static platform, baked to an offscreen canvas |
| `actors.ts` | Zuk, the glyph, the adds, the player |
| `overlay.ts` | glyph shelter, exposure lane, markers, countdowns |
| `renderer.ts` | `InfernoRenderer` — event→effect and scene assembly |

## All art is original

Every shape is generated by the code here: the basalt floor with its ember
cracks, the lava moat, the extruded rim, the spectator silhouettes,
TzKal-Zuk, the Ancestral Glyph, the four add types, the player marker and
the overhead prayer glyphs. **No Jagex/OSRS sprite, model, texture, icon
or font is copied, traced, extracted or reproduced**, and none is loaded
at runtime.

Zuk is an original silhouette assembled from primitives — a broad
volcanic mound, a narrower torso with shoulder and arm blocks, a head
block with a jagged crown, glowing seams and a charging core slot. He is
built to convey mass, state and charge, not to resemble the real model.
The adds are distinguished by silhouette and hue (lean archer, hunched
caster with an orb, hunched quadruped with a maw, drifting blob), again
without likeness.

## Why the engine grew a bigger snapshot

The rule that keeps the fight honest: **if a visual needs data, the engine
exposes it read-only — the renderer never recomputes it.** So
`SimSnapshot` now carries Zuk's anchor and footprint size, his charge
start tick, the glyph's patrol direction, per-entity `alive` / revive tick
/ next-attack tick / style, the player's move target, and the in-flight
input count.

The one piece of geometry this layer walks itself is the glyph's
sheltered tile set, and it is unit-tested tile-for-tile against the
engine's own `spanProtects` across a full patrol — drawing a safe tile the
engine calls exposed is the worst lie this layer could tell.

`updateSnapshot` also stopped rebuilding the entity array with
filter/map every tick; the views are pooled and refreshed in place.

## What the fight asks you to read

The Inferno is a positioning-and-prayer fight, not a dodge fight, so the
telegraphs are different from the Colosseum's:

- **Zuk's charge.** His core slot brightens across the 3-tick wind-up and
  a call-out over his crown shows one pip per remaining tick, labelled
  **SHELTERED** or **EXPOSED** from `playerBehindGlyph`.
- **The exposure lane.** His shot cannot be prayed or eaten through — the
  glyph is the only defence — so when he is charging and you are *not*
  sheltered, a lane lights up between him and you, brightening as the
  landing tick approaches, and your marker turns hot with a warning ring.
  That is the death cascade made visible.
- **The glyph.** The one cold-coloured object in the arena: a barrier
  pane on its row, the volume it shelters tinted below it, an integrity
  bar, cracks appearing under 60 %, and an arrow showing which way it is
  patrolling — i.e. which way to shuffle next.
- **Add windups.** Every add's ring is **blue for magic** and **green for
  ranged**, with a tick countdown, thickening on the tick it lands. That
  is the prayer-switch cue, and it mirrors an animation you can already
  see.
- **Healer beams.** Dashed lines from each Jal-MejJak to Zuk on the ticks
  they heal, so a boss health bar going the wrong way has a visible cause.
- **Revive timers.** A downed Jal-Zek keeps its bar and shows
  `REVIVES n`, because it is still a threat.

## Assists and honest reporting

Anything that makes the fight easier to *play* is off by default and
flagged afterwards:

- The four engine assists (`AssistOptions`: glyph safe-tile highlight, add
  timers, Jad prayer indicator, set countdown) are reported by
  `ResultsSummary.assistsUsed`, and the canvas shows an "N assists on"
  chip while any is active.
- Two render-side aids — the tile-coordinate grid and the tactical view —
  are listed by `visualAidsInUse()` next to the results.

Deliberately **not** assists, because they exist in game:

- The glyph and the volume it shelters. Both are physically there; hiding
  them would make the sim teach the wrong thing. The assist adds the hard
  green tile highlight and the exact boundary.
- The per-add windup ring and its colour, which mirror the attack
  animation. Showing an add's *idle* countdown before it winds up is
  gated behind `addTimers`.
- Zuk's charge pips, which mirror his core glow.

## Camera

Fit-to-platform by default, zoom 0.7×–1.6×, optional gentle follow. The
must-see rect is the union of the player, Zuk, the glyph span and every
live add; if the requested zoom cannot show it, the zoom is reduced until
it can. The camera is then nudged down so Zuk's art and his charge
call-out clear the top edge — his body stands well above his tiles, and
tile framing alone decapitates him.

The tactical view flattens to a square grid, which is the clearest way to
drill the one thing that matters most here: column alignment with the
patrolling glyph.

## Tests

`tests/inferno/render.test.ts` covers the pure parts under vitest's node
environment — no DOM:

- every tile round-trips `projectTile → screenToTile` on the 25×18 grid,
  in both views;
- the camera carries its own grid size, so the two arenas cannot alias;
- the glyph's sheltered tiles equal `spanProtects` exactly, across a full
  patrol, and never include a tile north of the bar;
- `chargeProgress` runs 0 → 1 across the declared window, clamps outside
  it, and falls back to the constant delay if only the land tick is known;
- Zuk is centred on his 7×7 footprint.
